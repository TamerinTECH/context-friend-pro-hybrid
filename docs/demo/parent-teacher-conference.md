# Demo meeting: parent–teacher conference (fictional)

**Everything here is invented.** Names, school, e-mail, phone number and dates are fictional
(`example.org`, `+49 30 0000000`). Two real people read it aloud near the phone; never use
text-to-speech for the demo, and never record a real meeting for it.

Reading time: about 2½ minutes. Read naturally, with small pauses between speakers.

**Speakers:** Mr Jonas Testmann (class teacher, year 4) · Ms Petra Sonnenberg (parent of Mila)

---

**Testmann:** Good afternoon, Ms Sonnenberg. Thank you for coming in. I wanted to talk about
how Mila is doing this term.

**Sonnenberg:** Thank you for making the time. I was a bit worried, to be honest.

**Testmann:** Let me start with the good news. Mila's reading has improved a lot since
September. She now reads aloud in front of the class, and last week she finished her first
chapter book on her own.

**Sonnenberg:** That's lovely to hear. At home she reads with her little brother every evening.

**Testmann:** It shows. Where she still struggles is maths, especially word problems. She
often knows how to calculate, but she gets nervous and gives up before she starts.

**Sonnenberg:** Yes, I see that at home too. And her homework takes far too long. Some
evenings it is almost two hours, and she ends up in tears.

**Testmann:** Two hours is too much for year four. It should be about thirty minutes. Let's
agree on something: if homework takes longer than forty minutes, please stop, and write a
short note in her homework diary. I will adjust the tasks.

**Sonnenberg:** That would really help. Can she get some extra support in maths?

**Testmann:** Yes. From next week, Mila can join the small maths group on Tuesdays and
Thursdays with Ms Beispiel. It is six children, and they work with objects and pictures first.

**Sonnenberg:** Good. Is there anything we can do at home?

**Testmann:** Short and positive. Ten minutes of maths games, three times a week, is better
than long worksheets. I'll send you a list of games by e-mail.

**Sonnenberg:** Please use petra.sonnenberg@example.org. And if anything urgent comes up, my
mobile is plus four nine, thirty, zero zero zero zero zero zero zero.

**Testmann:** Noted. One more thing: the school still has her date of birth as the twelfth
of March twenty fifteen, and the old address on Musterstraße twelve. Is that still correct?

**Sonnenberg:** The date is right, but we moved. I'll send the new address to the office.

**Testmann:** Thank you. So, to sum up: Mila joins the maths group from next week, homework
stops after forty minutes with a note, and I'll send the list of games. Shall we meet again
on the fifteenth of December to see how it's going?

**Sonnenberg:** Yes, the fifteenth works. Thank you, Mr Testmann.

**Testmann:** Thank you. Mila is doing better than she thinks.
